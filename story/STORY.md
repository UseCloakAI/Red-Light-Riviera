# LOWTIDE: Story Bible

Cloak Studios. Story bible, draft 1. The mission data that goes with it is in `story/missions.json`.

> Everything washes up eventually.

---

## 1. Logline and premise

**Logline.** A harbor pilot who lost her license when a container ship ran aground on her watch takes one night job for an old friend and finds the ship's "lost" cargo in the back of the van. In Marlowe Keys, everything she was blamed for is coming back in with the tide.

**Premise.** Three years ago the container ship *Ottoline* ran onto the Knuckle, the shoal at the mouth of the Marlowe Channel, with Hollis Vane on the bridge. The inquiry called it pilot error. The insurer paid out on eleven containers lost overboard. The channel stayed shut for 38 days, long enough for the shipping lines to move to the mainland and for the families of Port Salt to start selling their dock leases for cash in envelopes. Hollis stayed. She washes dishes at a truck stop across the causeway and lives on a sailboat on blocks in a boatyard she can't pay. When her friend Dez Ruelas offers $1,500 to drive a van to Pier 9 before the tide turns, she takes it, and the crates in the back are stenciled with the *Ottoline*'s hold numbers. The grounding wasn't an accident. It was a land deal. Working for a dock crew, a tired detective, a nervous motel owner and finally the developer himself, Hollis follows the cargo through the port, the police and the glass towers toward the one thing that can prove what happened that night: a small orange box on the bottom of the bay, which the lowest tide in forty years is about to give back.

The full timeline of the grounding is in Appendix A.

## 2. Themes and tone

### Themes

- **What you sink comes back.** Cargo, money, bodies, lies. Everyone in Marlowe Keys has put something in the water and hoped. The *Ottoline* cargo is the first thing to come back. It isn't the last.
- **Silence is a choice.** Every main character made one quiet decision to let something sink. Hollis stopped arguing. Pruitt typed what she was handed. Bernie lifted what came and didn't read the label. Dez took an envelope. Moss always votes yes. The crime story is about the loud things. The drama is about the quiet ones.
- **The tide is a clock.** The city lives by it and Hollis lives by it better than anyone. Missions run on tide windows: high water to get a boat out, low water to drive a road that isn't there at noon.
- **Who owns the waterfront.** The developers want the coast without the water: marinas, shade, "walkable." The people who work the water get a buyout envelope and a rendering of a park.

### Tone

- Grounded crime drama, told dry. People are funny because they're tired, not because they're trying. Nobody winks at the player.
- Violence is quick, loud and costs something. Deaths happen off screen or in one beat. Nothing lingers.
- No villain speeches. Ashby believes he's saving the city, and half the city agrees with him.
- Always dusk or night. Hot pink and orange going to deep violet over the water, sodium light on wet streets, pastel motels gone gray after dark. Signs are painted, not lit. The Seagrape's sign has been off since the bulb went. Florida-noir, not synthwave.
- Coastal in every line: tide, salt, piers, heat.

### How the tagline pays off

Act 1, the cargo washes up, literally, at Tower 4 on Tideline Beach. Act 2, the people wash up: Lionel's ledger, Rafi's errands, Bernie's deal. Act 3, the bay empties and the bottom comes up: Manny's skiff, the capsule, Dez's secret. The ending asks Hollis what to do with the last thing the water gives back. In one ending she brings it up herself. In the other she lets it sink again, and a year later it comes up anyway, at the same spot she said it would, with her name in it. Everything washes up eventually. The only choice is whether you're the one holding it when it does.

## 3. Protagonist: Hollis Vane

| | |
|---|---|
| Age | 41 |
| Was | A Marlowe Bar Pilot for ten years, the youngest ever licensed. Pilots board ships at the sea buoy and bring them through the channel. |
| Is | A dishwasher on the night shift at the Gannet Truck Plaza, on the mainland |
| Lives | Aboard the *Ida May*, a 30-foot sloop named for her mother, on blocks at the Salt Works Boatyard. The yard holds a $22,400 lien. |
| Look | Faded work shirt with the pilots' patch picked off, hair tied back with whatever was nearby |
| Voice | Flat, exact, few words. Gives times to the minute. |

**Who she is.** Hollis grew up in Port Salt, the daughter of a tug engineer. She knows every channel, cut and bar in the bay by feel and reads water the way other people read faces. Calm hands. She can drive anything with a wheel, and she shoots like someone who practiced alone.

**Why she's in Marlowe Keys.** She never left. Leaving would have been agreeing with them, and the boat doesn't float.

**What she wants.** $22,400 to get the *Ida May* off the blocks and sail out past the Knuckle for good.

**What she needs.** To say out loud what happened on the *Ottoline*, and to stay.

**Her flaw.** She would rather be blamed than argue. When someone tells her firmly what happened, she goes quiet and lets it stand. She says yes to whoever asks hardest, and she trusts tide tables over people.

**Her arc.** She gets the money at the midpoint and doesn't leave. She says yes to Ashby, and it costs her the one cop on her side. At the end, alone on the flats, she has to decide whether to finally say no.

**Sample lines**

1. "High water's 12:18. After that, nobody's leaving."
2. "I'm not a criminal. I'm between licenses."
3. "I didn't put that ship on the Knuckle. I was just the one standing on the bridge."
4. "It'll come up at Tower 4. The current hooks there."
5. "I say yes. It's a problem."

## 4. Main cast

### Dez Ruelas (Desmond)
- **Role.** Owns Ruelas Bait & Ice at the end of Tideline Pier. Moves small things through the port at night for a cut. Hollis's oldest friend, since they were kids in Port Salt. Gives the first job.
- **Look.** Fishing shirt, reading glasses on a cord, barefoot whenever he can get away with it.
- **Voice.** Quick and warm. Makes every job sound like a favor he's doing you. Can't stand a silence, so he fills it.
- **Wants.** To keep the shop, keep his people out of jail, and keep believing his brother ran off to the mainland.
- **Secret.** Three years ago he told Rafi where his brother Manny was hiding, for $20,000 and a promise that they'd only talk to him. He used the money to keep the shop.
- **Lines.** "You're late. Tide's already turning." / "Take this. It's for showing people."

### Calvin Ashby (antagonist)
- **Role.** Chairman of Ashby Tidewater, the developer behind Port Salt Commons. Chair of the Port Commission.
- **Look.** Linen suit, real sunburn, a smile he has professionally maintained.
- **Voice.** Warm and folksy. Never says a number out loud. Talks about the city like it's a boat he's captaining.
- **Wants.** Port Salt Commons built, and to be remembered as the man who saved the waterfront.
- **Secret.** He staged the *Ottoline* grounding: eleven containers dropped early for the insurance, and the channel blocked long enough to break Port Salt and buy it cheap.
- **Lines.** "I'm not threatening you. I'm describing you." / "Port Salt gave this city its name. We're going to give it a future."

### Detective Odette Pruitt (the cop who matters)
- **Role.** Marine Recovery and Found Property, Marlowe Keys Public Safety. Everybody calls it the Wash-Up Desk: anything that comes out of the water, from a cooler to a body, lands on her desk. A few months from retirement.
- **Look.** Rain jacket in all weather, reading glasses pushed up, a face like a bait shop after closing.
- **Voice.** Patient and level. Asks the second question first.
- **Wants.** To close one case properly before she goes.
- **Secret.** She wrote the inquiry finding that blamed Hollis. The Chief handed her the conclusion and she typed it.
- **Lines.** "Nobody calls it that to my face." / "Bring me something that floats."

### Bernie Szabo (Bernadette)
- **Role.** Night crane operator, union steward at Longshore Hall and boss of the Lanyards, the dock crew that takes a little out of every container.
- **Look.** Hi-vis vest over a church blouse, a lighter she never uses.
- **Voice.** Blunt, with a union-meeting cadence. Counts things out loud.
- **Wants.** The docks working and her people paid, whoever's paying.
- **Secret.** She craned the "lost" *Ottoline* boxes off a barge an hour before the ship grounded, and has fenced them for Ashby for three years.
- **Lines.** "I lift what comes. I don't read labels." / "I'm not sorry. I'm broke. It looks the same from outside."

### Rafi Castellan
- **Role.** Ashby Tidewater's Director of Community Relations, which means the muscle.
- **Look.** Pressed linen, loafers with no socks, even on a boat.
- **Voice.** Quiet and polite. Uses your title. Never raises his voice because he's never had to.
- **Wants.** Ashby's trust, and a house on Bellwether Key of his own.
- **Secret.** He killed Manny Ruelas at a meeting off the Knuckle and sank the skiff. He searched it in the dark and never found the capsule.
- **Lines.** "Ms. Vane. You're a long way from the water." / "Nobody ever is."

### Lionel Pike
- **Role.** Owns the Seagrape Motor Inn on Tideline Avenue. Treasurer of the Tideline Hospitality Association. Launders Ashby's buyout cash and carries it to Port Salt in envelopes.
- **Look.** Short-sleeved dress shirt, a tie with fish on it, a handkerchief that's given up.
- **Voice.** Nervous. Over-explains. Talks like a front desk.
- **Wants.** To keep the Seagrape, which hasn't made money since 1998.
- **Secret.** The motel's best customer is a shell company. Room 12 has been booked for three years and nobody has ever slept in it.
- **Lines.** "I'll be honest with you, which I don't usually do." / "Nobody's been a guest here since 1998."

### June Ashby
- **Role.** Vice president of sales at Ashby Tidewater. Runs the Port Salt Commons sales gallery. Calvin's daughter.
- **Look.** Sales-gallery blazer, sandals in her bag for after.
- **Voice.** Brochure-perfect until it isn't. Then very direct.
- **Wants.** Out from under her father, with the company.
- **Secret.** She wrote the insurance claim for the *Ottoline* cargo. She's turning on her father to take his chair, not to make anything right.
- **Lines.** "Can I get you a water? It's local." / "When this comes out, is my name in it?"

### Wade Purcell
- **Role.** Owns Purcell Salvage & Tow in Gannet Landing, on the mainland. Did the salvage on the *Ottoline*.
- **Look.** Coveralls tied at the waist, a sunburned neck, a dog that doesn't like him.
- **Voice.** Slow mainland drawl. One sentence at a time.
- **Wants.** To be left alone, and to pay off his barge.
- **Secret.** He picked up the eleven boxes on marker buoys before the *Ottoline* ever reached its pilot, for cash. He kept the receipts.
- **Lines.** "Keys are in it. They're always in it." / "I don't talk about that night. I tow things."

### Also in the city
- **Manny Ruelas.** Dez's younger brother, 26 when he vanished. Ran the pilots' launch the night of the grounding. Talked about fishing the whole way out.
- **Commissioner Eldon Moss.** The Port Commission's swing vote. Always a yes. Likes to be asked nicely.
- **Chief Warren Tibbs.** Chief of Public Safety. Fishes with Ashby on weekends.
- **Captain Teodor Brask.** Master of the *Ottoline*. Left the country. Heard only on the recorder.
- **Arlo.** Night cook at the Gannet Truck Plaza. Takes everyone back.
- **Tobias Fenn.** Runs the Salt Works Boatyard. Holds the lien on the *Ida May* and itemizes it.

## 5. Factions and power

**How power runs.** Ashby owns the Commission. The Commission owns the port. The port signs the Lanyards' paychecks. Public Safety owns the causeway. Everybody owes Lionel's motel a little.

| Faction | Who | What they want | Home turf |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ashby Tidewater** | Calvin and June Ashby, Rafi Castellan, Tidewater Security (polo shirts, earpieces, a lot of golf carts) | Port Salt Commons: condos, a marina and "real shade" where the docks are | Meridian Tower, Northpoint Marina, Bellwether Key |
| **Marlowe Keys Port Commission** | Five seats. Ashby chairs. Moss is the swing. | Whatever the chair wants, with a public comment period first | The Old Customs House |
| **The Lanyards** | Bernie Szabo and the night shift. Named for the port ID lanyards they never take off. | Steady work, a cut of every box, and the docks to still be there next year | Longshore Hall, Warehouse 6, the Ballast |
| **The Scrub** | A mainland crew from the pines past Gannet Landing. Car theft, arson, standing outside your house. | Whoever paid last | The Mill |
| **Tideline Hospitality Association** | The hotel owners. Meets in the Coquina's bar, mostly about parking. Lionel is treasurer, which is the problem. | Tourists, and fewer questions about Room 12 | Tideline Avenue |
| **Marlowe Bar Pilots** | The eleven pilots who bring every ship through the channel. Hollis was one. | Not to talk about Hollis | The pilot station at the sea buoy |
| **Purcell Salvage & Tow** | Wade Purcell, a barge, three wreckers and the dog | To be left alone | Gannet Landing |
| **Cormorant Mutual** | The marine insurer that paid $41 million. Offstage. | Its money back, if it ever finds out | Nowhere near here |

### The police: Marlowe Keys Public Safety

Locals just say **Safety**, as in "Safety's on the causeway." Sand-colored cruisers with a coral stripe; the light bars are the brightest thing on Tideline after dark. Headquarters is Central Station in Midtown. The Marine Unit and the Wash-Up Desk work out of Harbor Station in Port Salt. A causeway detail sits at the toll plaza. Chief Warren Tibbs runs the place and fishes with Ashby.

| Stars | What Safety sends |
|---|---|
| ★ | One patrol and a short search. Usually the causeway car, or whoever's nearest the diner. |
| ★★ | Two or three units and a wider search circle. |
| ★★★ | Roadblocks at the toll plaza and the main intersections. Marine Unit spotlights along the water. |
| ★★★★ | The Safety Response Team in unmarked trucks, and the helicopter, Gull One. |
| ★★★★★ | Everything. The Gannet Draw goes up and the causeway closes. Nobody leaves the island. |

Losing them: break line of sight, get outside the search circle, wait it out.

**Fail screens.** Death is **WASHED UP** (you wake at Saint Brendan's). Arrest is **NETTED** (you walk out of Central Station). A failed job is **JOB FAILED** with the mission's line under it, and two options: **Retry** and **Quit**. These are ours on purpose, so we don't borrow another series' screens.

## 6. World bible

### The lay of it

Marlowe Keys is an island in Marlowe Bay. Downtown is the north end. Midtown and Palm Square fill the middle. Port Salt is the west shore, on the channel. Tideline is the south beach, with its pier. The causeway runs east from the island across the bay to Gannet Landing on the mainland. Two small keys sit off the east side: Bellwether Key to the northeast, gated, and Gull Key to the southeast at the end of the Stilts. The Salt Flats run along the south shore from Gull Key to the Knuckle at the mouth of the channel.

The tide matters everywhere, and on the flats it's the whole map. Two highs and two lows a day. The Bar Road is above water for about two hours either side of low tide. The blowout in Act 3 is the only time in forty years the Knuckle is dry.

### Downtown
- **Vibe.** Glass towers that look at the water all day and never get wet.
- **Landmarks.** Meridian Tower, the tallest glass on the island, with Ashby Tidewater on the top six floors and the Port Salt Commons Sales Gallery in the lobby. Halyard Avenue, which locals call the Glass Mile. The Old Customs House, a coral-stone building squeezed between two towers, where City Hall and the Port Commission meet. Northpoint Marina: yachts, a fuel dock, Ashby's *Fair Weather* and the berth of the *Gilded Gull*, a casino day-boat that goes out three miles and comes back. Bellwether Bridge, with a guard hut and a gate arm, and across it Bellwether Key: twelve houses, one of them the Ashby house.
- **What happens here.** Money, meetings, valet parking. Open House, Public Comment's vote, Fair Weather, Gated Community, the start of House Money and Man Overboard.
- **Sound.** Flag halyards slapping on empty poles, rooftop air handlers, valet whistles, a fountain that actually runs.

### Midtown
- **Vibe.** Where the people who work Downtown live, and the people who work Tideline sleep.
- **Landmarks.** The Breakwater Diner, open 24 hours, cops at the counter and nurses in the booths. Keys Transit Depot. The Midtown Underpass, where the boulevard dips under the old rail embankment: no cameras, no shoulder. Saint Brendan's Hospital. Public Safety Central Station. The Coral Theater, whose marquee has said CLOSED FOR SEASON for eleven years. Banyan Street, where Commissioner Moss lives. Calder Street: bars, a laundromat, a pawn shop and double parking.
- **What happens here.** The city's kitchen and laundry. The hit in House Money, the chase in Public Comment, June's handoff at the Breakwater.
- **Sound.** Window units dripping on the sidewalk, buses kneeling at the curb, a ballgame on a radio behind a screen door, dogs behind chain-link.

### Palm Square
- **Vibe.** The city's front porch. Half the benches face the founder. Half face away.
- **Landmarks.** The Founder's Statue, Josiah Marlowe in bronze, facing the mainland; locals say it's the most honest thing in the city. The Fountain, dry since the drought of 2009, with a plaque about it. The Domino Tables under the banyan, where the old men know everything a day early. The Third Bench, Pruitt's office, unofficially.
- **What happens here.** Pruitt's meetings in Flotsam and Toll, dominoes, foot chases cutting through to Midtown.
- **Sound.** Dry palm fronds rattling, dominoes slapped down on concrete, sprinklers ticking on at midnight, an ice-pop cart bell.

### Port Salt
- **Vibe.** The city's working hands. Salted, cracked and about to be sold.
- **Landmarks.** Piers 1 to 8, under gantry cranes the locals call the Herons, standing in the shallows. The Pier 8 Barge Berth, where the night crane is Bernie's. The Stacks, the container yard, where every aisle looks the same, which is the point. Warehouse 6. Longshore Hall, the union hall: folding chairs, a coffee urn, Bernie's office. Gate 3. The Ballast, a dockworkers' bar, cash only. Harbor Station, home of the Marine Unit, the Wash-Up Desk and the evidence cage in the basement. The Salt Works, the old brick works and evaporation ponds the district is named for. Salt Works Road, running down past the ponds to Pier 9. The Salt Works Boatyard, where the *Ida May* sits on blocks.
- **What happens here.** Small Craft Advisory, the end of House Money, the break-in in Fair Weather, Last Shift, Bernie's Lifts.
- **Sound.** Container twistlocks clanking, crane reverse alarms, a ship's horn out in the channel, gulls fighting over a bin.

### Pier 9
The last numbered pier: old timber at the south end of Port Salt, where the docks give way to Tideline beach. Too shallow for container ships, so it's shrimp boats and people who don't want a receipt. At low water the pilings show rotted down to pencils. The T at the end hangs over the channel edge. The first job ends here, Rafi ends here, and under the Commons plan it becomes a marina with a juice bar. As Bernie says, everything ends up at Pier 9.

### Tideline
- **Vibe.** Pastel hotels with their best years in the brochure.
- **Landmarks.** Tideline Avenue, the beachfront strip of low-rise hotels and palms. The Seagrape Motor Inn: pink, two stories, a drained pool, an ice machine and Room 12. Hotel Coquina, the best hotel on the avenue, which isn't saying much. The Pelican Arms, mint-green, with a pool that's mostly leaves. The Starfish Court, bungalows around a parking lot. Tideline Beach, and Lifeguard Tower 4, where the current hooks into the sand and anything that goes in off Port Salt comes up. The Bandshell, concrete on the sand, summer concerts and winter pigeons. Bar Road Landing at the east end, where the Bar Road leaves the beach at low water.
- **What happens here.** Chum, Flotsam, Ice Run, Do Not Disturb, the end of Undertow, the Night Desk and most of Wash-Up.
- **Sound.** Surf, ice machines humming in breezeways, pool filters, pier boards creaking, a lounge piano two hotels down.

### Tideline Pier
The long fishing and amusement pier at the south end of Tideline Avenue. Rail fishermen with buckets, the shuttered Penny Arcade halfway out, and at the very end Ruelas Bait & Ice: bait, ice, a walk-in cooler and a back door onto the water. It burns in Undertow. In the Commons plan it's labeled "Phase Two."

### The Causeway
- **Vibe.** Four miles of concrete between what the island wants and what it needs.
- **Landmarks.** The Marlowe Causeway itself, low over the bay. The Causeway Toll Plaza at the island end, where a patrol parks for the air conditioning. The Causeway Catwalk along the south rail, where the regulars fish all night. The Gannet Draw, the drawbridge span near the mainland end, which Safety raises at five stars.
- **What happens here.** The patrol in Salt in the Wound, the roadblock in Soft Shoulder, the checkpoint in Toll, night fishing.
- **Sound.** Tires on expansion joints, wind with nothing to stop it, the draw bell, anglers' radios.

### The Mainland: Gannet Landing
- **Vibe.** Everything the island doesn't want to look at, at a fair price.
- **Landmarks.** The Gannet Truck Plaza, a 24-hour truck stop and diner with a car wash out back, where Hollis washes dishes. Purcell Salvage & Tow, on the barge canal. The Scrub, the pine scrub past the last streetlight, and the Mill, a burned-out lumber mill where the Scrub crew sleeps when it sleeps. The Gannet Deepwater Terminal, the new mainland port, half built, which took the shipping lines when the channel closed.
- **What happens here.** The opening, Soft Shoulder, hiding out after Undertow, the start of Toll.
- **Sound.** Diesel idling, cicadas, highway hiss, a dog barking at nothing.

### The Salt Flats
- **Vibe.** Half the day it's water. The other half it's evidence.
- **Landmarks.** The Bar Road, a shell track across the flats from Bar Road Landing to the Stilts. The Stilts, fishing shacks on pilings, and Manny's shack among them. Gull Key, mangroves, birds and mosquitoes. The Marlowe Channel, dredged from the sea buoy around the Knuckle into Port Salt. The Outer Anchorage, where ships wait for a pilot. The Knuckle, the shoal where the *Ottoline* sat for 38 days.
- **What happens here.** Undertow, Dead Low, the low-tide finds in Wash-Up.
- **Sound.** Mud ticking as it drains, wading birds, the channel bell buoy, wind over shallow water.

## 7. Story structure

Three acts, seventeen story missions. Objectives, dialogue, fail lines and rewards for every mission are in `missions.json`.

### Systems ladder

The first four missions work with on-foot movement, pedestrians, the pistol and the wanted system only. Mission 1 has an on-foot version for before vehicles ship. Vehicles arrive properly in mission 5.

### Act 1: On the Beach

Hollis needs money and takes jobs. The *Ottoline*'s lost cargo is back, and the grounding was no accident.

**1. Salt in the Wound**
- **Giver.** Dez. **District.** The Mainland, the Causeway, Port Salt.
- **Summary.** Dez pays $1,500 to drive a white van from the Gannet Truck Plaza across the causeway to Pier 9 before the tide turns. A patrol at the toll plaza runs the plate and Hollis loses it. At the pier a polite man in loafers takes the cargo: crates stenciled with the *Ottoline*'s hold numbers, three years after they were lost at sea.
- **Systems.** Driving, the wanted system at one star, a tide timer. **On-foot version:** the van died on Salt Works Road. Take the case out of it, lose the patrol in the Stacks and walk it to Pier 9. Same brief, same ending.
- **Plot.** The lost cargo isn't lost, and Rafi has seen Hollis. Two crates slide off the boat on the way out, which sets up Flotsam.

**2. Chum**
- **Giver.** Dez. **District.** Tideline.
- **Summary.** Dez hands Hollis a pistol "for showing people" and sends her to collect $3,000 Lionel Pike owes for ice. Two Lanyards are already in Lionel's office collecting for Bernie, and it turns into a fight in the Seagrape's courtyard. Lionel pays $800 and a story: he carries cash envelopes to Port Salt families for a Downtown company that wants their dock leases.
- **Systems.** On foot, pedestrians, the pistol, one to two stars.
- **Plot.** Introduces Lionel, the Lanyards and the buyout money.

**3. Flotsam**
- **Giver.** Pruitt. **District.** Palm Square, Tideline.
- **Summary.** The crates from Pier 9 come ashore at Tower 4, right where Hollis said they would. Detective Odette Pruitt of the Wash-Up Desk wants one, off the books, before the Lanyards' cleanup crew gets there, and the crew gets there early. Hollis carries a crate off the beach through Safety and sets it down next to Pruitt on the third bench.
- **Systems.** On foot, an optional photo, collect and carry (no sprinting with a crate), escape, two stars, deliver.
- **Plot.** Introduces Pruitt and the insurance: Cormorant Mutual paid $41 million for cargo "lost at sea." Pruitt has doubted the inquiry for three years.

**4. Small Craft Advisory**
- **Giver.** Bernie. **District.** Port Salt.
- **Summary.** Bernie Szabo wants to know who took her box and is almost pleased it was the pilot. Before she decides what to do about it, a Scrub crew comes over the fence with gas cans to burn Warehouse 6, and Hollis holds the door with the Lanyards. Rattled, Bernie shows her the Pier 8 barge log from the night of the grounding: eleven *Ottoline* boxes came off a Purcell Salvage barge at 3:40 AM. The ship hit the Knuckle at 4:52.
- **Systems.** On foot, defend in waves, eliminate the torch carriers, the pistol (a shotgun off Bernie's wall if it's in by then).
- **Plot.** The cargo was "lost" before the ship grounded. Someone is cleaning up, and Bernie is on the list. The trail points to Wade Purcell.

**5. Ice Run**
- **Giver.** Dez. **District.** Tideline.
- **Summary.** The first job with wheels: Dez's ice truck, four Tideline hotels and a load that's melting. Dez rides along and tells Hollis about his brother Manny, who drove her launch the night of the grounding, talked about a retirement plan, and left two weeks later in his skiff without a word.
- **Systems.** Getting in, driving, traffic, a chain of stops against a melt timer.
- **Plot.** Introduces Manny. Dez tells her to leave the *Ottoline* alone. She doesn't.

**6. Soft Shoulder**
- **Giver.** Wade (Hollis goes to him). **District.** The Causeway, the Mainland.
- **Summary.** Hollis drives to Purcell Salvage & Tow to ask Wade about the barge and gets there at the same time as the Scrub, who've been sent to close Wade's mouth for good. She holds the yard, gets Wade into his own wrecker and takes him back across the causeway through a Safety roadblock. Hidden on the *Ida May*, Wade tells her he picked up eleven boxes on marker buoys an hour before she ever went aboard, paid in cash by a man who wears loafers on a boat, on a contract with Ashby Tidewater's name on it.
- **Systems.** Survive while protecting Wade, a vehicle escape, three stars and a roadblock at the toll plaza.
- **Plot.** End of Act 1. The grounding was staged, and it was Ashby's. Purcell's yard becomes a safehouse.

### Act 2: Slack Water

Slack water is the still moment between tides. Hollis gets close to Ashby from every side. She gets what she wanted and finds it isn't what she needed.

**7. Open House**
- **Giver.** Pruitt. **District.** Downtown, Tideline.
- **Summary.** Ashby Tidewater is selling Port Salt Commons at an open house in the Meridian Tower. ### On the screen: WMK 6 and the late-night lineup

Every TV on the island is tuned to the same few channels, and they are louder than the city they broadcast to. The story is told dry; the television isn't. It's the one place in Marlowe Keys where people say the quiet part out loud, swear on air and get bleeped late. Played on sets in apartments, the Seagrape's rooms, bars, laundromats and the diner's corner TV, and on the Palm Square Theatre's screen. Shows and scripts: `lowtide/media/tv/shows.src.json`. How they're made: `lowtide/tools/tv/README.md`.

#### Dale Vance
- **Role.** Anchor, *WMK 6 News at Eleven*, for twenty-six years. The only local newscast left, which he takes to mean he can't be fired. He has been fired twice. They hired him back both times because nobody else would read the tide tables.
- **Look.** Silver hair that doesn't move, a navy suit, a tan from a bottle he keeps in the desk next to the other bottle.
- **Voice.** Low, slow, newsreader cadence that turns sour mid-sentence. Reads a story straight and then adds the one sentence his producer begs him not to.
- **Wants.** One clean night. A Gulf Coast Emmy. His second wife back, or failing that, her lawyer's number out of his phone.
- **Secret.** Ashby Tidewater pays for the eleven o'clock weather segment. Dale knows what that buys and reads the copy anyway.
- **Lines.** "Draw your own conclusions. I've drawn mine. They're in my lawyer's safe." / "Somebody get me a drink."

#### Bunny Marsh
- **Role.** First Alert weather, WMK 6. A meteorology degree from the mainland, a stage smile, and an open, cheerful wish that the next hurricane takes Bellwether Key first.
- **Look.** Blonde blowout the humidity is winning against, pinstripe suit, a clicker she points like a gun.
- **Voice.** Bright and fast, the voice of a morning-show host who has stopped caring who hears her. Says "honey" when she's about to be cruel.
- **Wants.** The network weather job in Atlanta. Failing that, to be on air when the big one hits.
- **Secret.** She's the one who leaks the tide warnings to Dez a day early. He pays her in shrimp.
- **Lines.** "Downtown, expect rain and gunfire." / "Dale, your family left you."

#### Chet Gaines
- **Role.** Spokesman for Tide Cola. Former Gulf Coast Conference running back, two championships, one acquittal. Says the last part himself now so nobody else gets to.
- **Look.** Built like a fridge, striped shirt open one button too far, a smile that took a dentist a year.
- **Voice.** Big, easy, salesman-warm. Every sentence lands like a touchdown he's still proud of.
- **Wants.** Another season. Any season.
- **Lines.** "Two time Gulf Coast champion. One time acquitted." / "Taste the tide. Damn right."

#### Tide Cola
- **What it is.** The island's cola, bottled at Tidewater Bottling in Port Salt, "with real Marlowe Keys water, mostly the clean part." Sweet, flat by the second sip, faintly salty. Thirty-five cents. Its ads carry a disclaimer longer than the ad. Tidewater Bottling is a subsidiary of a subsidiary of Ashby Tidewater, which nobody on the island has looked up.

#### Rex Fontaine
- **Role.** Host of *Spin City*, the island's only game show, filmed in an old bowling alley in Midtown. The wheel has one jet ski on it. It has never landed there.
- **Look.** Black dye job, veneers, a tan the color of a saddle, a cordless mic he never puts down.
- **Voice.** Rapid, sing-song showman patter. Insults contestants on the beat so the studio laughs on cue.
- **Wants.** His alimony paid (four ex-wives, all in the studio audience at least once), and his marker with the card room above the Nite Owl torn up.
- **Secret.** The wheel is rigged by a man under the stage with a brake lever. Rex calls him "the producers."
- **Lines.** "The only game show where the wheel is crooked, and so is your host!" / "I need a drink, and possibly a lawyer."

#### Darlene Sykes
- **Role.** *Spin City* contestant from Gannet Landing. Drove two hours. Lost.
- **Look.** Red bob, blue blouse, a lucky scrunchie on her wrist that is not lucky.
- **Voice.** High and nervous, then loud and righteous.
- **Lines.** "I'm so nervous I think I peed a little." / "This is rigged!"

#### Marty Kessler
- **Role.** Attorney at law, *Kessler & Associates*. There are no associates. Night-school law degree (by correspondence), an office over a dry cleaner on Ashby Boulevard, and an ad that runs every fifteen minutes after midnight because the slots are cheap.
- **Look.** White hair combed over nothing, a suit two sizes ago, a nameplate bigger than his desk.
- **Voice.** Fast, wheedling, a used-car rhythm. Starts every ad confident and ends it begging.
- **Wants.** One big case. Failing that, rent.

#### Captain Gull (Morty Plank)
- **Role.** *Captain Gull's Pier Patrol*, the island's kids' show: a gull puppet in a sailor cap teaching boys and girls about the ocean. Taped on a toy pier set at WMK in the afternoon, rerun at hours no child should be awake.

## 10. Endings

Dead Low ends at Bar Road Landing with the flood tide behind Hollis. Pruitt's car is on one side, alone. Ashby's truck is on the other. The last objective is "Choose who gets it." and it has two markers. Whichever one Hollis walks the capsule to is the ending.

### Ending A: Bring It Up

Hollis gives the capsule to Pruitt.

*INT. HARBOR STATION, THE WASH-UP DESK. Dawn. Pruitt plays the capsule on a borrowed reader. Hollis listens to herself.*

- **Hollis (recorder):** Port twenty, Captain.
- **Brask (recorder):** Midships. Hold her.
- **Hollis (recorder):** Captain, you'll put her on the Knuckle.
- **Brask (recorder):** Tell Mr. Ashby it's done.

*EXT. BAR ROAD LANDING. Ashby in cuffs, ankle deep in the mud the tide left.*

- **Ashby:** These shoes cost more than your car.
- **Pruitt:** It's a city car.

Chief Tibbs retires for family reasons. Pruitt retires on schedule, with the case closed. Hollis gets her hearing in the same room as three years ago, and this time she talks:

- **Hollis:** I said port twenty. I said it twice. Then I didn't say anything for three years. That part's on me.

Her license comes back. Dez pleads to his part in Manny's death and gets probation. Nobody asks too hard about Rafi. Manny is buried on the mainland, facing the water. June takes over Ashby Tidewater, settles with the city and builds the project anyway, renamed Ruelas Landing. The plaque spells Manny's name right.

*EXT. MARLOWE CHANNEL. Dusk, months later. Hollis climbs a pilot ladder up the side of a container ship at the sea buoy and brings it in past the Knuckle, which is only water again. On Pier 9 a crew is pulling pilings for Ruelas Landing. Dez is fishing off what's left. He lifts a hand.*

- **Helmsman:** Pilot's aboard.
- **Hollis:** Port ten. Easy.

*Card: LOWTIDE. Everything washes up eventually.*

**The payoff.** She went out and got it. Some things wash up on their own. Some you have to carry in.

### Ending B: Let It Sink

Hollis gives the capsule to Ashby and tells Pruitt there was nothing out there but Manny.

- **Hollis:** Don't put it in the channel.
- **Ashby:** It's a big channel.

*EXT. NORTHPOINT MARINA. The next night, Ashby drops the capsule off the stern of the* Fair Weather.

The pilots' board reinstates Hollis in a two-minute meeting. An envelope with $100,000 in it. Port Salt Commons goes up and Pier 9 becomes a marina with a juice bar. Dez leaves for the mainland. Pruitt retires with the case open. Hollis brings ships in at night, past the Knuckle, every night.

*EXT. TIDELINE BEACH, TOWER 4. One year later, low tide. A kid pulls something orange out of the wrack line.*

- **Kid:** There's an orange thing.

*INT. THE WASH-UP DESK. A new detective plugs it in.*

- **Hollis (recorder):** Port twenty, Captain.

*INT. A SHIP'S BRIDGE. Night. Hollis is bringing a ship in when her phone buzzes. Pruitt is retired, but she still hears things.*

## Appendix B: Glossary

- **Blowout.** An extreme low tide caused by days of strong wind pushing water out of the bay. Act 3's is the lowest in forty years.
- **The Herons.** The gantry cranes at Piers 1 to 8.
- **The Knuckle.** The shoal at the mouth of the Marlowe Channel.
- **Lagan.** Cargo sunk on purpose with a marker so it can be picked up later. What the eleven boxes were.
- **Pilot.** A licensed local mariner who boards ships at the sea buoy and brings them through the channel. Not the person who flies the plane.
- **Safety.** Marlowe Keys Public Safety, the police.
- **Slack water.** The still moment between tides.
- **The Wash-Up Desk.** Marine Recovery and Found Property. Pruitt's desk.
- **Wrack line.** The line of seaweed and junk the tide leaves on the beach. Where the city's secrets come back.

## Appendix C: Notes on `missions.json`

- Top level: `version`, `meta`, `acts`, `objectiveTypes`, `missions`, `endings`, `activities`, `ambient`, `radio`, `characters`, `factions`, `districts`, `places`.
- Each objective has `type`, `text` (the on-screen line), `place` (the place name), `placeId` and `district`, plus type-specific fields: `vehicle`, `item`, `count`, `seconds`, `target`, `distance`, `stars`, `timer`, `options`. `photo` objectives are always `optional: true`, so every mission plays without the camera.
- `dialogue[].when` is one of `start`, `end`, `cutscene_open`, `cutscene_end`, `on:<objectiveId>` (the objective begins), `done:<objectiveId>` (it completes) or `event:<name>` (a scripted trigger, listed in the mission's `triggers` where needed). Blocks can carry a `note` with staging. Lines heard on the recorder carry `source: "recorder"`.
- `needsVehicles: false` marks missions that run before vehicles ship. Mission 1 carries `variants.on_foot` for the same purpose.
- `requires` lists mission ids that must be complete first. The story runs in `order`.
- The last objective of Dead Low is a `deliver` with two `options`. Each names an ending id in `endings`.
